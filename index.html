Battling Botnets with Graph Neural Networks

A project by Naitik Sk

In today's digital landscape, the insidious spread of misinformation by automated "botnets" poses a significant threat. My recent project tackled this challenge head-on by developing a Graph Neural Network (GNN) to identify coordinated bot activity, using the Aditya Goyal Twitter Dataset. This wasn't just an exercise in coding; it was a deep dive into how advanced AI can protect our online spaces.

Conquering the KeyError
One of the project's most defining moments, and perhaps my most valuable learning, arose from a persistent KeyError. Despite meticulously moving my bot_detection_data.csv file, the Python script repeatedly failed, insisting that crucial data columns like favourite_count or Follower Count didn't exist. This wasn't a simple typo; it was a fundamental mismatch between my code's expectations and the dataset's actual column names.

Real-World Impact

This project's significance extends far beyond academic exercise. It demonstrates the power of GNNs in enabling crucial real-world interventions:

  • Coordinated Takedowns: By identifying entire networks of bots, platforms can move from individual account bans to simultaneously neutralizing whole botnets, significantly disrupting malicious campaigns.
  • Algorithmic Deprioritization (Shadow-Banning): Accounts flagged by the GNN can have their visibility reduced, mitigating their harmful influence without alerting the bot operators to their detection.
  • Adaptive Security: This approach showcases a future where social media defenses are dynamic, learning from evolving bot tactics to protect public discourse.
